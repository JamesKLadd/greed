Usage measurements

Greed sends optional first-party measurements to its own game server. They help us understand whether players finish practice, open or complete the account step for a daily challenge, try challenges, return, and use room invitations. Account-step measurements use the same random browser identifiers, without account details. Collection is on by default unless you turn it off or your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control.

Each record contains a fixed action name, random browser and session identifiers, a fixed source or campaign label, and the time it reached our server. It does not contain your name, email, account ID, score, dice, room code, messages, or full URL. A browser identifier expires after 30 days; a session expires after 30 minutes of inactivity. Measurement records are removed after 30 days by the server’s regular cleanup.

Only the game owner can view aggregate counts. These measurements do not change official rankings or results. There is no third-party analytics service or advertising tracker.

Usage choices are also available in the game’s Settings.

Turning measurements off clears their identifiers and stops future collection on this browser. Existing records expire through the regular cleanup. The choice is per browser; browser privacy signals take priority even if you choose to turn measurements on.

Your account and games

An account stores your username, email, protected password, profile, friends, and game results so you can sign in, play online, and enter ranked challenges. Completed daily results appear to players who have finished their own daily run; weekly standings are visible throughout the week. Match history and old challenge runs are kept for 30 days. Career totals and challenge wins are retained with your account.

Account deletion is available from Account in the game and requires your password. Deleting your account removes its server records. Local settings and device statistics are stored separately in your browser. Usage identifiers are separate from accounts; use the control above to stop those measurements.

Hosting and essential storage

Vercel hosts the web game, Render hosts the game server, and Neon hosts account data. Network requests reach those services and may appear in their operational logs. The usage table itself does not store IP addresses. Your device also stores login sessions, preferences, saved progress, and offline game files to make the game work.
